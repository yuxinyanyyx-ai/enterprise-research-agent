Systemic sclerosis (SSc) is a persistent autoimmune rheumatic disorder affecting multiple organs, with significant unmet needs in therapeutic options.
The pathogenesis of SSc is characterized by a complex interplay of vasculopathy, immune dysregulation and progressive fibrosis1–4
SSc is clinically heterogeneous and can affect the skin and several internal organs1,4,5

## Slide 6

Preclinical evidence on Nerandomilast in SSc
CONFIDENTIAL ONLY FOR INTERNAL USE
Pre-clinical data demonstrate that nerandomilast targets key pathogenic mechanisms of SSc — fibrosis, vasculopathy, and immune dysregulation — across relevant in vitro and in vivo models, with effects observed at clinically relevant concentrations. Importantly, these effects were not limited to the lung but also included skin and vascular tissues, supporting its potential for systemic disease modification.
Pre-clinical data show direct anti-fibrotic effects on lung and SSc skin fibroblasts and suppression of epithelial cell and keratinocyte pro-fibrotic activation
Potential to directly impact endothelial activation (literature and emerging in-house data) and indirectly through suppression of inflammation
Potential to directly attenuate skin fibrosis by inhibiting pro-fibrotic activation of keratinocytes and promoting barrier function
Potential to directly attenuate inflammation by modulating macrophage and lymphocyte activation and suppression of pro-inflammatory cytokines and IFNs (activities on macrophages are differentiated from MMF)

## Slide 7

Evidence: chunk-c77e84d0553e

Evidence from subgroup analyses in patients with SSc-ILD (trial 1305-0023)
CONFIDENTIAL ONLY FOR INTERNAL USE
The analysis of primary and key secondary endpoints in a subgroup of patients with SSc-ILD, though based on a relatively small sample size (n=75), provides compelling evidence supporting the potential efficacy of nerandomilast in this patient population.
In the SSc-ILD subgroup, nerandomilast showed numerically favorable trends in both primary and key secondary endpoints, despite the limited sample size. Safety data in this subgroup were generally consistent with the overall safety profile.
These findings support the clinical relevance of the pre-clinical data and justify further evaluation of nerandomilast on ILD and other organ manifestations in SSc in a confirmatory Phase III trial.

## Slide 11

Evidence: chunk-03f629109891

Summary
CONFIDENTIAL ONLY FOR INTERNAL USE
The combined non-clinical and clinical data provide a strong scientific rationale to initiate a Phase III trial of nerandomilast in SSc.
Pre-clinical data demonstrate that nerandomilast targets key pathogenic mechanisms of SSc — fibrosis, vasculopathy, and immune dysregulation — across relevant in vitro and in vivo models, with effects observed at clinically relevant concentrations. Importantly, these effects were not limited to the lung but also included skin and vascular tissues, supporting its potential for systemic disease modification.
Clinically, nerandomilast has demonstrated consistent efficacy and safety in two large Phase III trials in fibrosing ILDs (IPF and PPF). In the SSc-ILD subgroup, nerandomilast showed numerically favourable trends in both primary and key secondary endpoints, despite the limited sample size. Safety data in this subgroup were consistent with the overall safety profile.
These findings support the clinical relevance of the pre-clinical data and justify further evaluation of nerandomilast on ILD and other organ manifestations in SSc in a confirmatory Phase III trial.
